#### Problem 1:

Given a parabola and a line, we have to prove certain properties about their intersections.

1. Consider the line \( y = kx - b \) that intersects the parabola \( y = x^2 \) at points \( A \) and \( B \).
2. The intersection points of the line and parabola must satisfy:
   \[
   kx - b = x^2 \implies x^2 - kx + b = 0
   \]
3. Solving the quadratic equation, we find the x-coordinates of points \(A\) and \(B\):
   \[
   x_A = \frac{k - \sqrt{k^2 - 4b}}{2}, \quad x_B = \frac{k + \sqrt{k^2 - 4b}}{2}
   \]
4. The x-coordinate of the midpoint \(C\) of segment \(AB\) is:
   \[
   x_C = \frac{x_A + x_B}{2} = \frac{\left( \frac{k - \sqrt{k^2 - 4b}}{2} \right) + \left( \frac{k + \sqrt{k^2 - 4b}}{2} \right)}{2} = \frac{k}{2}
   \]
5. This shows that \( x_C \) depends only on the slope \( k \) and not on \( b \) (the y-intercept).

**Therefore, if two chords \( A_1B_1 \) and \( A_2B_2 \) are parallel, the midpoints of these chords have the same x-coordinate, meaning the line joining these midpoints is parallel to the y-axis. Hence, the perpendicular chord parallel to the x-axis and its midpoint lies on the y-axis, concluding that the directrix of the parabola is the y-axis.**

\boxed{}

#### Problem 2:

Given the function equation:

1. Let \( t = \frac{1}{x} \), then \( x = \frac{1}{t} \). The problem becomes:
   \[
   f\left(\frac{1}{t}\right) + \frac{5}{t} f(t) = \frac{3}{t^3}
   \]
2. Since this equality holds for any \( t \neq 0 \), it must also hold for \( t = x \):
   \[
   f\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) + \frac{5}{x} f(x) = \frac{3}{x^3}
   \]
3. These equalities form a system:
   \[
   \begin{cases}
   f\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) + \frac{5}{x} f(x) = \frac{3}{x^3} \\
   f(x) + 5x f\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) = 3x^3
   \end{cases}
   \]
4. To eliminate \( f\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) \), multiply the second equation by 5 and subtract it from the first equation:
   \[
   5x f\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) + 25 f(x) - \left( 5x f\left(\frac{1}{x}\right) + f(x) \right) = \frac{15}{x^2} - 3x^3
   \]
   Simplifying, we get:
   \[
   24 f(x) = \frac{15}{x^2} - 3x^3 \implies f(x) = \frac{5}{8x^2} - \frac{x^3}{8}
   \]

**Therefore, the function \( f(x) \) is:**

\[
\boxed{f(x) = \frac{5}{8x^2} - \frac{x^3}{8}}
\]

#### Problem 5:

To prove the expression is greater than 5 for \( 0 < \alpha < \frac{\pi}{2} \):

1. Consider:
   \[
   1 + \frac{1}{\sin \alpha} + \frac{1}{\cos \alpha} + \frac{1}{\sin \alpha \cos \alpha}
   \]
   Resulting expression without 1:
   \[
   \frac{\cos \alpha + \sin \alpha + 1}{\sin \alpha \cos \alpha}
   \]
   Let \( \sin \alpha + \cos \alpha = t \):
   \[
   \sin \alpha \cos \alpha = \frac{t^2 - 1}{2}
   \]

Left part becomes:
   \[
   \frac{t + 1}{\frac{t^2 - 1}{2}} + 1 = \frac{2(t + 1)}{t^2 - 1} + 1 = \frac{2}{t - 1}
   \]

Need to prove \( \frac{2}{t - 1} > 2 \):

   \[
   \frac{2}{t - 1} > 2 \implies t < 1.5
   \]

Given:
   \[
   \sqrt{2} \approx 1.41 < 1.5
   \]

The proof is complete.

#### Problem 6:

   \[
